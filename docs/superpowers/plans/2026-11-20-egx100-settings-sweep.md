# EGX100 settings sweep: baseline vs one change at a time

Generated by `tools/backtest/sweep.mjs --data data\egx` on 2026-11-20. Universe: all 100 stocks in `data/egx` (split-adjusted only, full history, no holdout). Trades still open at the end of the data are not counted (14 at most per config).

**Base** = preset `tv-base`: daily, Long Trades Only, Apply Costs on (0.1% slippage per side + 2 EGP per order, position 100,000), Min Risk (ticks) 10, everything else at the Pine default. Each row changes only what its name says.

**How the numbers are made.** Per closed trade: net return % = net R x |entry - initial stop| / entry x 100 (same formula as `validate-picks.mjs`; it is net of costs and blends the three scale-out thirds). Win = net return > 0. All money figures are percent of position, one trade = full notional (position size is irrelevant). Max drawdown is peak-to-trough of the cumulative **sum** of trade returns (in % points), trades ordered by exit date, starting from 0. Avg R is shown for reference only.

## Caveats

- **In-sample.** Every row uses all the data, 2023+ and pre-2023 alike, and the 18 stocks that used to be held back are included. Picking the best row of this table is picking on the same data it was measured on; it is a description, not a validation. The earlier out-of-sample verdicts (Require Confirmation failed, runners passed the final re-test, the three stop picks went through the pick rule) stand on their own records. Rows that win only in one period, or only because of a handful of charts, should be read as noise until a fresh sample says otherwise.
- **Split-adjusted data only.** `data/egx` is adjusted for splits but not dividends, while the TradingView charts the Pine script is judged on are dividend-adjusted. Price level drives the Min Risk (ticks) floor and the commission term, so early trades differ from what TradingView would show (see `tools/backtest/README.md`). Results here are the port on this feed, not a TradingView replay.
- **% at full notional is not risk-normalised.** A return in % of position equals net R x the stop distance in % of entry, so a setup with a wider stop produces bigger % wins, bigger % losses and a bigger % drawdown for the same R behaviour (see the Avg initial risk column). Rows with wider stops (3, 4, 5, 16) are flattered on Net profit % and Avg return % relative to Avg R, and penalised on Max DD %; compare Avg R and Profit factor for the quality of the edge, and the % columns for what a fixed-notional position would have returned.
- **Survivorship.** The universe is the stocks listed today; delisted or suspended names are absent, which flatters every row roughly equally.
- **Sum-of-% is not an equity curve.** Net profit and drawdown add percent-of-position returns trade after trade as if every trade used the same full notional and nothing compounded; Max DD is the drawdown of that running sum. A real account sizes by risk and cannot hold 99 stocks at full notional at once.
- **Trades overlap across stocks.** Dozens of trades are open on the same days, and EGX stocks move together with the index, so trades are not independent: the effective sample is smaller than the trade count and the drawdown figure understates the pain of a bad market month.
- Costs: 0.1% per side + 2 EGP per order are included in every return. Wins/losses are on the net return (a "win" after costs).


## Fair comparison at fixed risk, and the reading (lead, after the Opus check)

An Opus 5.5 check recomputed five rows independently, and every column matched. Its caveats:
- The % columns assume a full-notional position. That inflates the wide-stop rows (3, 4, 5, 16;
  the swing-low stop averages ~20% of price) by about 2.5×. Those rows are flattered in the
  "beats base in both periods" column too.
- Trades opened before 2010 supply 37% of the baseline's net %.
- Every config's max drawdown falls before 2023.

test remaining. Require Confirmation (row 15) looks good here but failed out of sample on batch
D (2026-11-03).
